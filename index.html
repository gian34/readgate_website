What trees are really made of

Plants build themselves largely out of air.

It is easy to assume a tree comes mostly from soil. In fact, most of its mass comes from the air. Through photosynthesis, a plant pulls carbon dioxide from the air around it. Using sunlight as energy, it combines that gas with water. The result is sugar, the plant's basic fuel.

From that sugar, the plant builds wood, bark, and leaves. Soil still matters, but mainly for water and minerals. The bulk of the tree itself is captured carbon. A tall trunk in a forest is, in a real sense, captured air. It got turned solid by nothing more than sunlight and time.

A towering tree is largely thin air, caught and made solid by sunlight.

A few things to know.

Good questions.
Simple answers.

How does Readgate protect an app?

Readgate uses Apple's Family Controls and Managed Settings frameworks. Choose a target, and iOS shows Readgate's reading gate when you try to open it. Readgate does not receive your general Screen Time history.

Can I use Readgate for free?

Yes. Free includes one protected app or website, unlimited reading unlocks, a 10 minute window, reading history, and weekly stats. Pro adds more protected apps, schedules, flexible timing, and full Readgate Originals.

Where do the reads come from?

Readgate combines bundled Insight Cards and Readgate Originals with curated open knowledge from sources such as Wikipedia and Wikivoyage. Source links are shown where applicable, and bundled content remains available offline.

What happens after I finish reading?

Readgate lifts its gate for a limited unlock window. You can use the protected app during that break. When the window ends, the reading gate returns.

Which devices are supported?

Readgate is made for iPhone and requires iOS 18 or later.
