What trees are really made of

Plants build themselves largely out of air.

It is easy to assume a tree comes mostly from soil. In fact, most of its mass comes from the air. Through photosynthesis, a plant pulls carbon dioxide from the air around it. Using sunlight as energy, it combines that gas with water. The result is sugar, the plant's basic fuel.

From that sugar, the plant builds wood, bark, and leaves. Soil still matters, but mainly for water and minerals. The bulk of the tree itself is captured carbon. A tall trunk in a forest is, in a real sense, captured air. It got turned solid by nothing more than sunlight and time.

A towering tree is largely thin air, caught and made solid by sunlight.

A few things to know.

Good questions.
Simple answers.

How does Readgate protect an app?

Choose an app or website, and Apple's Screen Time frameworks show Readgate's reading gate when you try to open it. Readgate does not receive your general Screen Time history.

Can I use Readgate for free?

Yes. Free gates one app or website with unlimited reading unlocks, and its reads come from Surprise me: a fresh idea every time. Chapter 1 of every course is free too. Pro opens every course from start to finish, puts a reading gate on every app you choose, and adds schedules, flexible timing, Quick access and every reading series.

Where do the reads come from?

Readgate combines bundled Insight Cards with curated open knowledge from sources such as Wikipedia. Network content is fetched directly from the relevant source when needed. Source links are shown where applicable, and bundled reading content remains available offline.

What happens after I finish reading?

Completing a reading gate opens all your selected apps and websites for the same unlock window. Free gives you 10 minutes, and Pro lets you choose your timing. When the window ends, reading gates apply again according to your schedule. Reads opened from Discover do not grant an unlock.

Which devices are supported?

Readgate is made for iPhone and requires iOS 18 or later. The current release is in English.
